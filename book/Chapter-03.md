# Chapter 3: Describing Verbs

This chapter expands the past particle + pronoun + verb pattern by adding the
verb phrase's two modifier slots, and shows that the words which describe an
action are themselves verbs. A describing verb follows the action verb, as in
*Sā ʻou foʻi fiafia* ("I returned happily"); the want word *fia* stands before
it, as in *Sā ʻou fia alu* ("I wanted to go").

---

## The Describing Verb

Compare the sentence taught already with the same sentence plus one word:

::: {.examples}
*Sā ʻou foʻi.* I returned.

*Sā ʻou foʻi fiafia.* I returned happily.
:::

Compare them. Which word is new, and what does it add?

The new word is *fiafia*, and it sits straight after the verb. *Foʻi* means
"return"; *fiafia* means "happy", and here it tells you how the returning was
done: happily, in a glad way. Nothing else in the sentence moved. The past
particle *sā* still stands first, the person word *ʻou* still sits in the middle,
and the action verb *foʻi* still leads the pair. The describing word follows
it.

In English, words like "happy" and "strong" are adjectives, a separate kind of
word from verbs. In Samoan, the words that describe an action are themselves
verbs, the same kind of word as *alu* and *foʻi*. So *fiafia* (happy) is a verb,
and when it follows another verb it does a describing job: it says how the
action was done.

The pattern has the action verb first and the describing verb second, both inside
the frame you know:

| Samoan | English |
|---|---|
| *Sā ʻou foʻi fiafia.* | I returned happily. |
| *Sā ia siva fiafia.* | She danced happily. |
| *Sā ʻe eva fiafia.* | You strolled happily. |
| *Sā ia tagi mālosi.* | She cried hard. |
| *Sā ʻoutou siva lelei.* | You all danced well. |

*Mālosi* means "strong", and after an action verb it says the action was done
with strength, which is the job it is doing in the fourth sentence above.
*Lelei*, the word for "good" you already carry, does the same describing job in
the fifth, and the same is true of the word for "happy" in the first three. A
describing verb sitting after an action verb tells you how the action was done.

> **Preview:** When more than one person is involved, a describing verb can change
> its shape to match, the way an action verb does. Those plural forms are taught
> in Chapter 19.

> **Preview:** These describing verbs will also attach to the names of things once
> you have them, a happy child or a deep hole, and there each one works like a
> small sentence of its own. That fuller picture is completed in Chapter 40.

## The Want Word *Fia*

The second thing this chapter adds is the word for "want". It joins the same
frame, but it stands in a different place: in front of the action verb, not
after it.

::: {.examples}
*Sā ʻou fia alu.* I wanted to go.
:::

*Fia* is the word for wanting to do something, and it sits directly before the
verb. The pattern: the past particle *sā*, then the pronoun, then *fia*, then
the verb. The describing verb of the last section came after the action; *fia*
comes before it. The same order holds for every verb taught so far, down to
*fesili* (ask) in the last row:

| Samoan | English |
|---|---|
| *Sā ʻou fia alu.* | I wanted to go. |
| *Sā ia fia siva.* | She wanted to dance. |
| *Sā mātou fia fāgota.* | We wanted to go fishing. |
| *Sā ʻe fia foʻi.* | You wanted to return. |
| *Sā ʻou fia fesili.* | I wanted to ask. |

> **Preview:** With the past particle *sā*, *fia* says "wanted". To say "want" as
> something felt right now, in the present, Samoan sets a different particle in
> front of the sentence. You meet it in Chapter 13. Until then, every *fia*
> sentence you build is about what someone wanted. The same short word does a
> second job elsewhere, asking "how many", and that use waits until Chapter 22.

## Words to Learn

**New grammar words** (memorize how these work):

| Word | What it does |
|---|---|
| *fia* | want: goes in front of the verb and says the action was wanted |

**New vocabulary** (memorize these meanings):

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *fiafia* | happy, glad (after a verb: happily) |
| *mālosi* | strong (after a verb: hard, with strength) |
| *foʻi* | return |
| *tagi* | cry |
| *fesili* | ask |

**Words to carry** (learned now, used fully later in this book): *fou* (new)
and *loloto* (deep) describe things rather than actions, and they join a
sentence fully once the names of things arrive later in this book; *tau*
(reach) needs a place to reach, which also comes later. That word has other
senses too, one of them a word that sits before the verb, not after it, and
means "almost", so meet it here as "reach" and expect the others in time.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *fou* | new |
| *loloto* | deep |
| *tau* | reach |

## Exercises

### Exercise 1: Translate into English

1. *Sā ia siva fiafia.*
2. *Sā ia tagi mālosi.*
3. *Sā ʻou fia alu.*
4. *Sā ʻe eva fiafia.*
5. *Sā ʻe fia foʻi.*

### Exercise 2: Translate into Samoan

1. I returned happily.
2. She wanted to dance.
3. You all danced well.
4. We wanted to go fishing.
5. I wanted to ask.

### Exercise 3: Add the describing word

Rewrite each sentence with the describing
verb in brackets in its correct place.

1. *Sā ia siva.* (fiafia)
2. *Sā ia tagi.* (mālosi)
3. *Sā ʻou foʻi.* (fiafia)
4. *Sā ʻe eva.* (fiafia)
5. *Sā ʻoutou siva.* (lelei)

### Exercise 4: Put in *fia*

Rewrite each sentence to mean that the action was
wanted, not done.

1. *Sā ia alu.* (She wanted to go.)
2. *Sā mātou fāgota.* (We wanted to go fishing.)
3. *Sā ʻe siva.* (You wanted to dance.)
4. *Sā ʻou foʻi.* (I wanted to return.)
5. *Sā lātou alu.* (They wanted to go.)

### Exercise 5: Before or after?

In each sentence one word is out of place for the meaning given. Write
the corrected sentence.

1. *Sā ʻou fiafia foʻi.* (meaning: I returned happily)
2. *Sā ia siva fia.* (meaning: She wanted to dance)
3. *Sā mātou mālosi tagi.* (meaning: We cried hard)
