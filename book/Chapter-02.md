# Chapter 2: Preverbal and Independent Pronouns

So far, two pronouns have filled the slot between the past particle and the
verb: *ʻou* (I) and *ia* (he, she). This chapter completes the preverbal
pronoun set, every person and three numbers, as in *Sā ʻoutou siva?* ("Did you
all dance?"), and introduces the independent pronouns, a second set for the
two jobs the preverbal forms cannot do.

---

## The Pattern

The frame is the one taught already: the past particle *sā*, then the word for
who did it, then the verb. That word for who did it is a **preverbal pronoun**,
the kind met as *ʻou* and *ia*, so named because it stands before the verb.
Only that middle word changes. Here is the same slot with the full range of
people Samoan can put in it.

::: {.examples}
*Sā ʻou alu.* I went.

*Sā lātou alu.* They went.
:::

Nothing about the frame moved. *Sā* still stands first, *alu* still stands last,
and the word between them still says who did it. What grows in this chapter is
the list of words that fill that middle slot.

### One Person

| Samoan | English |
|---|---|
| *ʻou* | I |
| *ʻe* | you (one person) |
| *ia* | he, she |

### More Than One Person

These words for who did it mark number three ways, not two: besides one and
many, they have a form for exactly two. And when a group includes the person you
are speaking to, Samoan uses a different word than when it leaves that person out.

| Samoan | English |
|---|---|
| *tā* | we two (you and I) |
| *mā* | we two (but not you) |
| *tātou* | we (you and I, and others) |
| *mātou* | we (others and I, but not you) |
| *ʻoulua* | you two |
| *ʻoutou* | you all |
| *lā* | they two |
| *lātou* | they |

The forms glossed (you and I) include the person spoken to; the forms glossed
(but not you) leave that person out. The first kind is called inclusive, the
second exclusive.

Two of these forms sit a single sound apart. Read them aloud:

::: {.examples}
*Sā tātou siva.* We danced.

*Sā mātou siva.* We danced.
:::

Compare them. Which word changed, and does the English translation show the
difference?

The word changed from *tātou* to *mātou*, and the plain English "we" hides what
Samoan makes plain. *Tātou* pulls the listener in: we, you included. *Mātou*
shuts the listener out: we, but not you. Samoan asks you to choose between them
every time you say "we".

> *Note:* A way to keep them straight: *tātou* takes you in, *mātou* leaves you
> out. The choice carries real weight. Say "we ate" with *mātou* and you have
> told your listener they had no part in it.

> *Note:* The word for "he" or "she" has a second form, *na*, which is the form
> required in one construction taught later in this book. Every sentence in this
> chapter uses *ia*.

**Quick Practice A.** Put the person into the frame, using *alu* (go). Answers
below the block.

1. they (more than two)
2. you two
3. we, you and I
4. we, but not you

Answers: 1. *Sā lātou alu.* 2. *Sā ʻoulua alu.* 3. *Sā tātou alu.*
4. *Sā mātou alu.*

## Independent Pronouns

The words so far all stand in one place: the middle slot, right in front of the
verb. That is the only place they can stand, and in it they are usually the one
doing the action, never the one the action is done to. Almost every other job
takes a second, separate set, the **independent pronouns**. The one exception is
saying whose something is: that has a third set of its own, taught in Chapter 10.

> *Note:* A pronoun in the middle slot is never the one on the receiving end.
> English draws a similar line with "I" and "me": "I" does the action, "me" is on
> the receiving end. Samoan draws the same line, but between two whole sets of
> words and by position. Several forms are the same word in both sets, so
> position is what separates them. The moment a pronoun is on the receiving end,
> or takes any job outside that middle slot other than saying whose something
> is, the independent set takes over.

Two jobs call for it now.

### Saying Who It Is

To say who someone is, set the presenting word *ʻo* in front of an independent
pronoun:

::: {.examples}
*ʻO aʻu.* It is me. (Lit. "I")

*ʻO ʻoe.* It is you. (Lit. "You")
:::

*Aʻu* is the independent form standing for "I", *ʻoe* the independent form
standing for "you". The middle-slot *ʻou* could never do this: it cannot leave
its slot in front of a verb. The full pattern for saying who or what something
is comes later in this book. For now, carry *ʻo aʻu* and *ʻo ʻoe* as ready-made
ways to say "it is me" and "it is you".

### Pronouns After *Ia Te*

The other job is standing after the word that points toward people and places.
In front of a person-word that word has a shape of its own: the pronoun takes
its independent form and the word appears as *ia te*:

::: {.examples}
*ia te aʻu*. to me, towards me

*ia te ʻoe*. to you, towards you
:::

Once more the independent forms *aʻu* and *ʻoe* do work the middle-slot forms
cannot. The place words that point toward people and places are taught in full
later in this book; *ia te aʻu* and *ia te ʻoe* are the pieces you need now.

> **Preview:** Alongside the everyday "I", Samoan carries a second first-person
> form that colours a sentence with feeling, used to show humility or to appeal
> for help. You meet it in Chapter 51.

> **Preview:** A middle-slot pronoun can be paired with its independent twin in
> the same sentence for emphasis, the way English leans on "I myself". The full
> pattern is taught in Chapter 34.

## Words to Learn

**New grammar words** (memorize how these work). The preverbal pronouns, one
person first:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *ʻou* | I |
| *ʻe* | you (one person) |
| *ia* | he, she |

Then the forms for more than one:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *tā* | we two (you and I) |
| *mā* | we two (but not you) |
| *tātou* | we (you and I, and others) |
| *mātou* | we (others and I, but not you) |
| *ʻoulua* | you two |
| *ʻoutou* | you all |
| *lā* | they two |
| *lātou* | they |

The independent forms and the two words that call for them:

| Word | What it does |
|---|---|
| *aʻu* | me (the independent form of "I") |
| *ʻoe* | you (the independent form of "you") |
| *ʻo* | the presenting word: puts forward who it is |
| *ia te* | the form the place word takes before a person-word |

**New vocabulary** (memorize these meanings):

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *sau* | come |
| *eva* | go for a stroll |
| *minoi* | move |

**Words to carry** (learned now, used fully later in this book): *sasa* (hit)
and *maua* (get) each need someone or something on the receiving end, and the
words that mark that receiver come later in this book.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *sasa* | hit |
| *maua* | get, obtain |
